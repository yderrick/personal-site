Started the thing that eventually became Worldbuilder, though at this point it was just a project
called AIstudio and a strong suspicion that the writing software I wanted didn't exist.

Got the skeleton up in one sitting: project structure and dependencies, then a Tiptap editor so there
was somewhere to actually put prose. After that, the parts that made it more than a text box — scene
management in the manuscript workspace, a storyboard, and tracking for point of view and character
status.

The idea I was chasing even this early: the manuscript and the notes about the manuscript should be
the same system. Writing a scene should know which characters are in it. Most tools make you keep
those in two places and reconcile them yourself.
