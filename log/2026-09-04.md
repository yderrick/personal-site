Twenty-five commits, four releases, v2.33.0 through v2.41.0.

Wrote down a standing constraint I had been holding informally: AI in this app reads, it does not
write. It can tell me two characters sound identical; it cannot rewrite their dialogue. The whole
point of the project is that the prose is mine. A rule you only hold in your head isn't a rule, so it
went in the repo.

Under that constraint, the AI pass fills the gap the rules engine declines — labelling dialogue it
can't attribute confidently. Which meant tolerating what models actually return: numbers spelled as
strings, punctuation that doesn't match the source. Forgive a model's punctuation, not its vocabulary
— if it invents a word that isn't in the text, that's a hallucination and the result gets dropped.
Rate limits are treated as "not yet" rather than as failure. And when a run fails, the console says
what the model actually replied instead of a generic error.

Then the idiolect check: record how a character speaks, and have the app flag lines that don't match.
It needed something to check against first, so recording came before checking. A related fix — a
rewrite could reach an analysis surface by its shape rather than its wording, which meant edited prose
was being matched against a stale reading.

Also added a daily word-count goal that keeps a day's count and its baseline together across two
tabs, a pass that names setups you forgot to mark with one click to mark them, ranking interaction
partners by who a character actually speaks to rather than who they appear near, and Show all / Hide
all on the Relation Graph category filter.
