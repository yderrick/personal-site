Spent the day getting Daylog from nothing to something that actually records.

Scaffolded it as a Compose app sitting on top of a pure-Kotlin core module. Keeping the core free of
Android imports was the one structural decision I made up front, and it paid for itself immediately —
the chunk-boundary logic is the fiddliest part of the whole thing, and being able to test it without
an emulator in the loop made it a normal afternoon instead of a miserable one.

Before writing any of it I checked the thing most likely to sink the project: whether the device
would actually hand me the audio I needed. It did. Better to find that out on day one than after a
month of building around an assumption.

Then the capture path proper — the foreground service, the manifest declarations it needs, and
enough UI to start and stop a recording. Verified on-device rather than in the emulator, because the
emulator's audio stack has lied to me before.

Two things I'd tell myself at the start of the day:

- Resolving the ambiguous decisions in writing *before* opening the editor made the actual
  implementation almost mechanical. The brief took twenty minutes and saved more than that.
- Pushing at the end of each phase rather than at the end of the day. It's off-machine backup more
  than it is version control, and I've lost work to a dead laptop before.

Still minimal. It records, it stops, it writes a file. That's the whole feature set.
